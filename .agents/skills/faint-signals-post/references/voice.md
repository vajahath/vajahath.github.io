# How Vaju actually talks

Real samples. Read these before drafting. They are the ground truth for the
voice — more reliable than any rule written about them.

## Sample 1 — Sept 2026. Register: frustrated but constructive.

He is unhappy with how the blog reads and is explaining what he wants instead.
Annoyed, but not ranting — he keeps stopping to clarify so he isn't
misunderstood.

> I want the blog post to be a bit imperfect. Currently, they are too much
> cringey and I don't like the current taste. That is not how I write the post.
> I want you to understand how I would write from the transcription I give you,
> and I want you to match the transcription style of that blog post.
> Transcription in the sense, I'm talking to you right now in verbal terms, so
> the transcriber in my computer is actually converting those to actual words.
> So what you currently see in this prompt is my own words as coming out of my
> mouth.
>
> This is how I speak, and I speak from a very high-level standpoint, and often
> I dig deeper, and then you can feel I'm going into deeper things. And I also
> bring the listeners with me as I go deep.
>
> And the current blog posts in this blogging site is not actually reflecting my
> speaking or writing style. They are too much in a reporting style, too much in
> a too much cringey way. I don't like that. I like a little bit unperfection
> because that is how humans talk, right? They're not perfect.
>
> And this doesn't mean I want 'uh', 'oh', those words in the blog post. I'm not
> saying that, I'm not requesting that. But what I want is a human touch from
> the transcription I gave you. So currently, if you look at the blog posts,
> those posts do not have a soul behind it.
>
> The titles, they don't belong to me. They don't belong to my writing sense.

### What to notice

- **"right?"** — he turns to the reader mid-thought and checks they're with him.
- **Repetition for emphasis** — "too much in a reporting style, too much in a
  too much cringey way". He leans on a word when it matters. The written version
  keeps one beat of this, not all three.
- **Pre-empting the misunderstanding** — "And this doesn't mean I want 'uh'...
  I'm not saying that." He anticipates being taken too literally and heads it
  off. That instinct belongs in the posts too.
- **Short declaratives stacked up** — "I don't like that." "They don't belong
  to me."
- **Plain vocabulary throughout.** The biggest word in the whole passage is
  "reflecting". Nothing here needs a second read.
- **States the principle, then the feeling behind it** — "I like a little bit
  unperfection because that is how humans talk." Reason attached to opinion,
  always.
- **High level, then down.** He opens with the complaint in one line, then
  works into what specifically is wrong, then into titles. Wide to narrow.
