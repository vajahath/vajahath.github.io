In a historic move that many are calling a "Geneva Convention for the Digital Age," the Global Artificial Intelligence Consortium (GAIC) officially ratified the Ethical AI Deployment Framework today. The agreement, signed by 45 nations and 20 of the world's largest technology firms, sets a new international standard for how artificial intelligence should be developed, tested, and integrated into society.

The framework focuses on three core pillars: transparency, accountability, and human-centric design. Under the new rules, developers must provide "interpretability reports" for large-scale models used in critical sectors like healthcare, law enforcement, and finance. Furthermore, a new international body will be established to monitor compliance and mediate disputes related to cross-border AI impacts.

"We are standing at a crossroads," said Sarah Chen, the lead coordinator for the GAIC. "This framework ensures that as we move into an AI-driven future, we do so with our eyes open and our values intact."

Critics, however, argue that the framework lacks strong enforcement mechanisms and relies too heavily on voluntary cooperation from tech giants. Despite these concerns, the announcement has been met with optimism from the global community, sparking a rally in tech stocks as investors anticipate a more stable regulatory environment.
